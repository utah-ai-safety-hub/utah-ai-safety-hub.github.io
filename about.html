About

The Utah AI Safety Hub is a new, independent, and nonpartisan community for people in Utah who want to help make advanced AI safe and beneficial.

Our mission

Help people in Utah learn about AI safety, find each other, and start contributing, whether through research, policy, engineering, or other work.

Why now

AI is advancing faster than the number of people working to make it safe. Most AI safety work happens in a few cities, but many capable people aren’t going to move to San Francisco or London, and they shouldn’t have to. Local hubs are how those people find their way in.

How we work

We start small, use proven curricula and borrowed spaces, and work alongside groups that already exist in Utah rather than duplicating them. We welcome newcomers and people from across the political spectrum.

Why local hubs work

Hubs in Buenos Aires, London, Singapore, Toronto, and New York show how this works. Most began with a handful of people running a reading group in a borrowed room. Over time they grew into communities that train new researchers, host fellowships, and give people a place to work. As Li-Lian Ang argues in “We need more local AI safety hubs”, talent comes first. Funding and good strategy follow from it.

Why Utah

Policy

An active state government

In 2024 Utah created the Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy, the first office of its kind in the United States. States are where much of today’s AI policy gets made.

Research

Strong universities

The University of Utah, BYU, Utah Valley University, and Utah State train thousands of computing and policy students, and alignment research is already happening on campus.

Industry

A growing tech scene

The Wasatch Front has a large and fast-growing community of engineers and founders, many of whom build with AI every day.

Who runs it

Emilio Barkett is an AI safety researcher who studies the behavior of large language models. He is starting the Utah AI Safety Hub to give people in Utah a local way into the field.

We’re looking for a few more people to help organize. If that sounds like you, sign up and tick “Volunteering.”

Questions

Do I need a technical background?

No. AI safety needs people from many fields, including policy, law, communications, operations, and education. Our first reading group is designed for newcomers.

Does it cost anything?

No. Our reading group, co-working, and events are free.

Do I have to live in Salt Lake City?

No. We’re starting in Salt Lake City and hope to add groups in Utah County and on other campuses. Tell us where you are when you sign up so we know where to grow.

Are you affiliated with a university, company, or political party?

No. The hub is independent and nonpartisan. We hope to work with universities, community groups, and others across Utah.

How can I help?

Come to an event, bring a friend, or help organize. Tick “Volunteering” on the sign-up form and we’ll be in touch.
