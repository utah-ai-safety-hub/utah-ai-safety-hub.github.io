About

The Utah AI Safety Hub is a new, nonpartisan community for people in Utah who want to help make advanced AI safe and beneficial.

Why local hubs matter

Most AI safety work happens in a few cities. But many capable people who care about how AI goes aren’t going to move to San Francisco, London, or Washington, and they shouldn’t have to. Local hubs are how those people find each other, learn the field, and start contributing.

Hubs in Buenos Aires, London, Singapore, Toronto, and New York show how this works. Most began with a handful of people running a reading group in a borrowed room. Over time they grew into communities that train new researchers, host fellowships, and give people a place to work. As Li-Lian Ang argues in “We need more local AI safety hubs”, talent comes first. Funding and good strategy follow from it.

Why Utah

Policy

An active state government

In 2024 Utah created the Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy, the first office of its kind in the United States. State leaders are actively working on how AI should be governed, and states are where much of today’s AI policy gets made.

Research

Strong universities

The University of Utah, BYU, Utah Valley University, and Utah State train thousands of computing and policy students, and alignment research is already happening on campus.

Industry

A growing tech scene

The Wasatch Front has a large and fast-growing community of engineers and founders, many of whom are building with AI every day.

How we work

  • Start small. We use proven curricula and borrowed spaces before building anything new.
  • Welcome newcomers. You don’t need a technical background or prior knowledge of AI safety.
  • Stay nonpartisan. AI safety matters to people across the political spectrum, and we want everyone at the table.
  • Build with others. We want to work alongside existing groups in Utah rather than duplicate them.

Organizer

Emilio Barkett is an AI safety researcher who studies the behavior of large language models. He is starting the Utah AI Safety Hub to give people in Utah a local way into the field.

We’re looking for a few more people to help organize. If that sounds like you, sign up and tick “Volunteering.”
